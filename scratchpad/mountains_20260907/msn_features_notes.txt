msn_features.npz -- per-quadrant features from the vanilla terrain LOD normal
maps.  Sub-lane of RECOVER.  Everything below is MEASURED unless a line says
`reasoned`.  Load with allow_pickle=True; `names` is an object array.

    M     float32 (192, 192, 4, 31)   [cy+96][cx+96][quadrant][feature]
    have  bool    (192, 192)
    names object  (31,)

Quadrant order is the ESM's: 0 BL, 1 BR, 2 TL, 3 TR.  Nothing is normalised.


WHAT WAS SWEPT
--------------
All 2,304 `Commonwealth.4.<x>.<y>_msn.DDS` tiles, 512x512 BC3_UNORM, 10 mips,
at MIP 0 -- so a cell is 128x128 texels and a quadrant is 64x64 = 4,096 texels.
603,979,776 texels in total, 367 s.  Channels are decoded signed as
(v/255 - 0.5)*2 with R = nx east, G = ny up, B = nz north.

Neighbourhood operators (laplacian, box residuals, first differences) run over
the WHOLE 512x512 tile before the quadrants are cut out, not inside each 64x64
quadrant.  A quadrant's edge texels therefore see their real neighbours in the
adjacent cell rather than a truncated window; only the outer 4-texel ring of a
whole tile is edge-padded.  This differs deliberately from rec_features.py,
which truncates per quadrant.


THE 31 COLUMNS
--------------
   0-2   mean_nx  mean_ny  mean_nz        per-channel mean (mostly geometry)
   3-5   std_nx   std_ny   std_nz         per-channel std
   6-8   lap_nx   lap_ny   lap_nz         mean |3x3 laplacian|      <- narrowest band
   9-11  hp9_nx   hp9_ny   hp9_nz         std of (channel - 9x9 box mean)
  12-14  hp5_nx   hp5_ny   hp5_nz         std of (channel - 5x5 box mean)
  15-17  dh_nx    dh_ny    dh_nz          mean |horizontal first difference|
  18-20  dv_nx    dv_ny    dv_nz          mean |vertical first difference|
  21     rough                            mean angle (rad) between a texel normal
                                          and the quadrant's mean normal
  22     aniso                            Eh / (Eh + Ev), gradient energy summed
                                          over channels.  Bounded form of the
                                          brief's horizontal/vertical ratio, so a
                                          flat quadrant cannot divide by zero.
                                          0.5 = isotropic.
  23     lapVec                           mean || 3-vector laplacian ||
  24     tiltMean                         mean sqrt(nx^2 + nz^2)
  25     tiltStd                          std of that
  26     hpTilt                           std of (tilt - its 9x9 box mean)
  27-29  detRat_nx/ny/nz                  lap / (std + 1e-4): scale-free
                                          speckliness, so a flat-but-gritty and a
                                          steep-and-gritty quadrant score alike
  30     hpCorr_xz                        correlation of hp9(nx) with hp9(nz)

Two high-pass widths are kept on purpose.  The geometric normal comes from the
33x33 VHGT grid at 4 texels per vertex, so the 3x3 laplacian (2-texel band) is
the closer approximation to pure material, while the 9x9 residual keeps
material plus the finest geometry.  The covariate-shift table decides which.


GATES
-----
1. DECODE -- PASS.  bcnp.decode_rgb vs dds.py at mip 0 on four _msn tiles
   (0.0, -40.-40, 60.60, 16.-24): 0 mismatching channels, max abs diff 0 on
   every one.  Alpha is min 255 max 255 on all four, so the RGB-only vectorised
   path loses nothing.  `python msn_decode_check.py`

2. RESHAPE SELF-TEST -- PASS.  The whole-tile 8x8-quadrant reduction is
   compared against a naive per-quadrant recomputation of the means, stds,
   roughness and tilt on 9 quadrants of 2 tiles: worst difference 7.8e-08.
   `python msn_selftest.py`

3. ORIENTATION -- PASS.  Independent end-to-end check that my reshape landed
   the features on the right cell: correlate per-quadrant tiltMean against the
   per-quadrant VHGT mean slope from features.npz over 71,888 quadrants.
       as written                       r = +0.6036
       quadrants N/S swapped in cell    r = +0.5039
       cell rows flipped (cy mirrored)  r = +0.2553
       cell cols flipped (cx mirrored)  r = -0.1819
   `python msn_orient_check.py`

4. GREEN SANITY -- the stated convention holds, emphatically.
       mean(ny_up)          = 0.9363
       fraction ny_up > 0   = 0.999998   over 603,979,776 texels
   Green is up.  Not a hint of the usual up-in-blue tangent-space layout.

5. COVERAGE -- 36,864 of 36,864 cells got features.  `have` is all True.

SURPRISES, IN ORDER OF HOW MUCH THEY MATTER
-------------------------------------------
A. THE COLUMN THAT ACTUALLY IDENTIFIES MATERIAL IS THE ONE THAT DOES NOT
   TRANSFER.  A one-way F-ratio screen over painted quadrants owned >= 70% by a
   single material (2,766 quadrants, 17 materials) ranks the up-channel
   high-pass columns first, and their class means run in exactly the physical
   order you would want:

     LOceanFloor01              lap_ny 0.021   (smoothest)
     LCoastSandWet01                   0.031
     LRiverbedSilt01Wet                0.035
     LRiverbedSilt01                   0.045
     LDirtGravel01                     0.054
     LMarshMudFloor01                  0.061
     LCoastSandDry01                   0.074
     LGlowingSeaMud01                  0.082
     LGlowingSeaRubble01               0.088
     LBlastedForestDirt01              0.092
     LGlowingSeaRubble01NoGrass        0.106  (roughest)

   That is not noise.  Ocean floor smooth, rubble rough, gravel between them --
   the hypothesis in the brief is CORRECT IN KIND.  But lap_ny scores overlap
   85.3% against the 90% bar, and every one of its siblings lands at 82-87%.
   The single most informative normal-map column is the one the transferability
   rule forbids.  It misses on overlap, not on shift (-0.43).
   `python msn_probe.py 0.70 20`

B. THE TRANSFERABLE COLUMNS ARE HALF A SMUGGLED ELEVATION PROXY.  Correlating
   each transferable column against all 22 colour/terrain features over all
   147,456 quadrants, the strongest partner is nearly always hMean:

     hp9_nz +0.668   hp5_nz +0.665   dh_nz +0.609   hpTilt +0.609
     lap_nz +0.572   dv_nz  +0.566   hp9_nx +0.516  lapVec +0.451
     hp5_nx +0.464   dh_nx  +0.240   dv_nx  +0.227  lap_nx -0.208
     mean_nx +0.150  mean_nz +0.139  aniso +0.124   hpCorr_xz -0.017

   hMean was REJECTED by rec_shift.py (overlap 83.8%, shift +0.66).  `reasoned`:
   a column can pass the shift gate on its own marginal and still be doing part
   of its work through a variable that does not transfer, so the nz-family
   columns should be trusted less than their own overlap numbers suggest.  The
   four with |r| <= 0.25 against everything colour knows -- mean_nx, mean_nz,
   aniso, hpCorr_xz -- are the clean ones, and they are also the weakest.
   `python msn_indep.py`

C. THE FINE DETAIL IS REAL DATA, NOT BC3 ARTEFACT -- for nx.  mean |laplacian|
   of nx is 0.62 on a channel whose whole range is [-1, 1], which looked at
   first like an encoder tantrum.  Testing it: mean |first difference| ACROSS a
   4x4 block boundary over the same WITHIN blocks, with the diffuse tiles as
   the "real content, same compressor" control:

     msn nx                 h 1.11   v 1.10
     msn ny                 h 1.55   v 1.58
     msn nz                 h 1.38   v 1.57
     diffuse lum (control)  h 1.10   v 1.09

   nx sits exactly on the control.  ny and nz carry a visible block signature
   because their amplitude is smaller and the 5/6-bit endpoint quantisation is
   relatively larger there.  `python msn_blockcheck.py`

D. THE CHANNELS ARE NOT SYMMETRIC AND I CANNOT EXPLAIN IT.  East (nx) is about
   twice as rough as north (nz) at texel scale everywhere: lap 0.62 vs 0.30,
   dh 0.201 vs 0.095, hp9 0.204 vs 0.120.  Both are 5-bit endpoints in BC1
   colour, so it is not a bit-depth difference, and terrain has no reason to
   prefer an axis.  Comparing measured mean|laplacian| against what white noise
   of the same std would give, nx is at 67% of white and nz at 42% -- nx is
   nearly unstructured at texel scale while nz is not.  This is why lap_nx has
   the highest overlap of any column (97.0%) and the LOWEST F-ratio of the
   detail columns (32.1): it transfers perfectly because it is close to noise.
   Flagging it rather than explaining it.

E. THE FAR TERRAIN IS MEASURABLY SMOOTHER, THE SAME WAY IT WAS IN THE DIFFUSE.
   Every detail column shifts negative out of bounds (hp9_nz -0.45, hpTilt
   -0.54, std_nz -1.04, rough -1.11).  This matches rec_shift.py's finding that
   the far diffuse is smoother (laplace -0.42 sd), so it is a property of the
   region, not of my operators.  It biases detail matching toward the smoother
   materials out of bounds.

F. mean_ny has only 44.3% overlap although its shift is only +0.45.  The far
   distribution is bimodal -- flat procedural filler plus the steep border
   mountains -- so its 1-99 band sticks out of the painted band on both sides
   at once.  Overlap and shift disagree here; overlap is the honest one.


WHETHER IT IS WORTH ANYTHING (measured, beyond the brief's ask)
--------------------------------------------------------------
Same k-NN, same folds, same labels as rec_holdout.py; only the columns differ.
The colour+ row reproduces the parent lane's 28.4% exactly, which is the check
that my pipeline is comparable to theirs.

  OUTWARD (inner core -> outer rim)      LTEX top-1   texture-group top-1
    colour+     (14 cols)                   28.4%          29.0%
    msn only    (16 cols)                   25.0%          25.2%
    colour+msn  (30 cols)                   30.1%          30.5%

  BLOCKED (5 folds, 12-cell blocks)
    colour+                                 39.3%          39.8%
    msn only                                28.2%          28.7%
    colour+msn                              39.7%          40.3%
  `python msn_holdout.py`

The decay test is the one that matters, because rec_decay.py showed colour's
28.4% was mostly spatial autocorrelation.  Deep 50% rim holdout:

  dist to train      n     colour+   msn only   colour+msn
      1 - 2         808     43.9%      28.7%      43.6%
      2 - 4        1433     43.3%      28.0%      41.6%
      4 - 8        2406     38.3%      29.7%      39.6%
      8 - 16       2526     23.6%      21.9%      23.4%
     16+            515     11.3%      13.4%      13.8%
  `python msn_decay.py`

The normal map decays FLATTER than colour (28.7 -> 13.4, a factor of 2.1,
against colour's 43.9 -> 11.3, a factor of 3.9) and BEATS colour outright
beyond 16 cells.  That is the signature of a feature that is describing the
material rather than describing where it is, and it is the strongest evidence
in this lane that the brief's hypothesis was right in kind.

It is still not enough.  At 16+ cells colour+msn reaches 13.8% against the
18.5% null of "always assign the most common material", and the real recovery
targets sit up to ~60 cells out.  Adding every transferable normal-map column
moves the honest OUTWARD number from 28.4% to 30.1%, and the far-range number
from 11.3% to 13.8% -- from clearly worse than a constant guess to still worse
than a constant guess.


BOTTOM LINE
-----------
The `_msn` does carry baked material normal detail; it is not compression
noise; it orders materials by physical roughness correctly; and it generalises
with distance better than colour does.  It does not make the recovery work.
`reasoned`: the two facts are consistent -- the discriminating band is the
up-channel high-pass, which is exactly the part that fails the transferability
gate, so what survives the gate is the weaker half of a signal that was already
too weak.
