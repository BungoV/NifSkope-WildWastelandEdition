## The work
1. **Measure vanilla's erosion statistics first**, on TILING2's 22 sheets, from vanilla's `_msn` (through TILING3's mapping): the slope-magnitude spectrum and band table; the channel statistics (rill spacing and width from the autocorrelation of the high-pass across-slope; the flow-direction coherence -- how well the high-pass gradient aligns with the coarse downslope direction, which is what makes it FLUVIAL rather than noise); the amplitude of the fine normal perturbation vs the coarse slope (does erosion scale with slope, as real drainage does?). Floors: white noise of the same SD; the phase twin. Ceiling: vanilla vs vanilla across neighbouring sheets. These numbers are the target the pass is fitted to.
2. **The pass**: on the chunk's height field upsampled to bake resolution (bicubic from the 128-u grid, plus a small deterministic seed noise so channels can form), run a hydraulic erosion (droplet or shallow-water flow with sediment capacity, thermal term optional) for N iterations at fixed parameters; the output is a fine height delta, from which the fine normal (into the `_msn` writer) and a slope/flow/deposition mask (into the colour: shading term and, if TILING3's D verdict says vanilla's colour follows slope, a material tint for exposed rock vs deposit). Fit N, rain, capacity, seed amplitude to item 1's statistics on both tiles; report the fit table with the floors. Cost: wall time per chunk at 512 and at 2048 (ours is 2048 per chunk on the stock path -- state which resolution the pass runs at and what it costs on the Commonwealth's chunk count; if it is minutes per chunk, say so and offer the coarser pass with the number).
