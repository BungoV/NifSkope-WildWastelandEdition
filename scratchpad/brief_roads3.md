## What is known going in
- Ours after ROADS2: road texel = the asphalt diffuse's whole-texture average, opaque, hard-edged. - The seam bungo still sees: in the after-panel the road is TWO-TONE, a lighter outer band around a darker core -- the skirt / edge pieces (the 76 vertex-alpha-ramped shapes, and any separate edge material) are each painted as their own flat average colour, so the boundary between skirt and surface is a step. ROADS2's seam metric (piece-boundary discontinuity) went green while this step stayed, so that metric did not measure what bungo sees: measure the CROSS-ROAD luminance profile (edge to centre, per texel) on vanilla vs ours -- vanilla's must be read first; a step in ours where vanilla is smooth is the seam. The skirt's vertex alpha is the obvious candidate for the missing ramp (ROADS2 found vanilla's luminance does not FOLLOW the alpha, but that was measured as a correlation over the whole road; a ramp confined to the skirt's width is a different test).
- So the two candidate laws for vanilla's road are (A) the road composited at PARTIAL opacity over the ground, `v = a * roadAvg + (1 - a) * ground`, with `a` well under 1 and soft at the edge; (B) an opaque road whose sheet was then filtered/downsampled by the bake (which would also soften every other edge in the sheet -- TILING2's edge survey answers that). Possibly both. The hue (blue-grey) may be the road's diffuse under vanilla's grading (GRADE1's curve applied to the asphalt colour -- compute what GRADE1's curve does to the asphalt average and compare), or a lighting term; measure before naming.
