THE VERDICT, and it is not the one the ruling assumed.

A LAMBERT SHADING OF THAT DETAIL READS ZERO. The three coefficients sit
at the phase-twin floor on every sheet (median R^2 0.0000 against a twin
floor of 0.00001) and their SIGNS FLIP sheet to sheet -- kU runs -1.3376 to
+0.4461 -- which is what a fit to noise looks like. The fit machinery is
not at fault: the known-answer control recovers an injected 0.5 as
0.5000. A Lambert dot simply cannot SEE a rill -- a rill`s two walls
tilt opposite ways and their dots cancel.

THE CURVATURE OF THAT SAME DETAIL DOES NOT READ ZERO. Its divergence --
a crevice-darkening term, dark in the channel, light on the ridge --
reads r -0.1046 median, the SAME SIGN on 7 of 7 sheets, beating its own
phase twin on 7 of 7. It is the same column TILING3 section 2 found at
the top of a 22-column basis, and it is the term this lane ships.

So the ruling`s INTENT is delivered -- vanilla`s own fine geology reaches
the diffuse, rills and drainage where vanilla has them -- by the term
that measures rather than the term that was named. What it recovers is
about 1.1 % of the colour`s fine variance (median R^2 0.01095), not most
of it, and this lane does not claim otherwise. TILING3 section 2 already
showed why no per-texel law can do better: vanilla`s colour grain SHARES
ITS ORIENTATION with the `_msn` (agreement +0.27..+0.32 against twin
floors near +0.03) but is not a per-texel FUNCTION of it (22-column
ceiling R^2 0.018..0.023).
