## What is known going in, and the hypothesis to test
- TILING2 proved, with a validated instrument, that vanilla's fine detail (SD 4.5-5.5 luminance levels, everything finer than 5 texels) correlates with NOTHING at fixed phase: not the land textures at any mip, not VCLR, not slope, not directional shading. Fixed-phase correlation is the wrong instrument for a signal whose PHASE is random.
- **Hypothesis A (stochastic tiling):** vanilla's bake samples the same land textures with the phase broken -- a random offset and/or rotation per texel or per tile (hex-tiling, per-cell rotation, jittered UVs, or the same texture at a different tiling than the engine's 341.3333). Such a term has the land textures' amplitude spectrum and colour histogram and zero correlation with any fixed-phase copy -- exactly TILING2's table. Test it by the two statistics that survive random phase: (1) the radial amplitude spectrum of vanilla's high-pass residual vs the spectrum of the land-texture composite at the footprint mip with its phase randomised per texel / per tile (TILING2's `phase_twin` of the composite is that comparator for free) -- and, separately, vs white noise of the same SD (the floor: if vanilla's residual is spectrally white, it is noise, not texture); (2) the histogram / higher moments (skew, kurtosis) of vanilla's residual vs the same for the phase-randomised composite vs Gaussian noise. A texture-shaped spectrum with texture-shaped moments = hypothesis A.
- **Hypothesis B (a noise texture):** vanilla ships `Textures/Terrain/Noise.dds` (1024 x 1024, DXT1, 11 mips; director looked 22:2x). The engine's own strings carry `LandLODParamsPS/VS` and `MTLandLODBlend` but no LOD-noise setting name the director could find in a two-minute grep; a runtime multiply would NOT be in the sheet, but a bake-time one would. Test it as a fixed-phase candidate over a scale search (the noise tiled at 1/2/4/8/16/32 cells and at 341.3333 u) against vanilla's residual with TILING2's `t4_corr.py`; a correlation above the twin floor at any scale names it. Also read `Textures/Terrain/Noise.dds`'s own spectrum against vanilla's residual (a spectral match without a phase match would mean the noise is applied with random phase or rotated per cell).
- **Hypothesis C (a coarser bake resampled):** vanilla baked at a finer resolution with the land textures at the engine's repeat, then downsampled with a filter that leaves the texture's aliased grain but no coherent repeat. Test: the residual's spectrum vs the composite baked at 4x resolution then box/lanczos-downsampled; its periodicity at lag 10.67 must be inside vanilla's law.
- **Hypothesis D (geology from a finer heightfield -- bungo 22:2x: "the diffuse of vanilla lod land textures, it looks like there's variety to it, some geological features shown"): TEST THIS FIRST.** Vanilla's fine detail has DIRECTION (streaks, ridges, drainage lines, rock patches) finer than the LAND grid (one vertex per 128 u = 4 texels at dim 4) can carry; vanilla's `_msn` carries ~11x our texel-scale detail with no source in the LAND heightmap either (PIC-CHUNK, ROADS1 1.4). Hypothesis: Bethesda baked from a finer, erosion-simulated source heightfield that is not shipped, and the colour's fine detail is shading (and/or slope-driven material) from that surface. TILING2's slope and shading candidates read zero because they came from OUR 128-u grid. Test: correlate vanilla's colour high-pass residual against a directional shading of VANILLA'S OWN `_msn` high-pass (best of 8-16 azimuths, and a Lambert-with-sky term), and against its slope magnitude; floors = the phase twin of the `_msn`, and our `_msn` under the same shading (must stay at zero, as TILING2 found). A correlation above the floor names the source as the finer surface. Then the split: how much of vanilla's residual variance is the shading term vs what remains (texture grain or noise -- A/B/C on the remainder).
  If D holds, the design choice (bungo's call, proposed default first): (1) `--land-detail-source vanilla`: where a shipped sheet exists for the chunk, take vanilla's own high-pass residual (colour, and the `_msn` high-pass for TERRAINFMT1 later) as the detail layer over our composite, scaled by the fitted strength -- Bethesda's geology kept, ours where it is not; (2) `--land-detail-source erosion`: a deterministic procedural erosion pass on our heightfield (seeded by world position, byte-reproducible, 1 vs 16 threads identical) shaded the same way, for worldspaces that ship no LOD -- prototype it on one tile and show its spectrum and moments beside vanilla's; (3) none. Ship (1) with (2) as the fallback unless bungo rules otherwise; both behind switches, off == rung bytes.
- One of A/B/C/D must fit both the spectrum and the moments on both tiles and on 5 of TILING2's 22 sheets, with the floors beside it; if none does, the lane REFUSES with the table and ships nothing new (a refused round is a deliverable) -- but then it ALSO says what "as close as we can get" costs: the best-looking of the three on the same statistics, marked as not vanilla's law.
